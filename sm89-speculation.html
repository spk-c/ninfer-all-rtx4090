DFlash2 and MTP on SM89: draft width, VRAM and measured throughput

A practical interpretation of the exact v0.1.4 short Engine benchmark and its limits.

Why draft width matters

Speculative decoding asks a model drafter to propose tokens for target-model verification. Wider windows may reduce verification rounds but cost extra work and memory. DFlash2 and MTP are alternative, model-dependent backends; acceptance and tokens/s are different measurements.

Exact test conditions

Huihui IQ3_S, RTX 4090, published v0.1.4, 2,048 prompt tokens and 128 timed decode tokens; 32K capacity, rk4v4, GDN FP16, two measured repetitions and one warmup. N-gram copy disabled. These are Engine measurements, not HTTP throughput or coding-quality scores.

Context and cache

Long prompts increase KV storage and prefill work; prefix reuse changes TTFT. Main KV budgeting alone is not a VRAM guarantee: drafter, recurrent state, workspaces, allocator and desktop also consume memory. Compare identical prompts, seed, cache state and output length at longer contexts.

Reproducible tuning

Begin with a conservative 32K profile and K5, check outputs, TTFT and memory, then test K3/K5/K7/K9 individually. K7 wins this short corpus, but K9 has lower acceptance and throughput. No blanket default change. Bonsai MTP tests use different weights and n-gram width 15; they are not a direct quality or acceptance ranking.
